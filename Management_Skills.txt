Before teaching live, instructors should learn to

Failure Scenario.1
Instructor explains a concept, Students understand quickly.
There are still 25 minutes left:
The instructor starts repeating the same explanation.
The instructor ends the session early
What Failed:
The instructor treated extra time as a
problem instead of a learning
opportunity.

-- 8 of 37 --

Depth Expansion Technique
When extra time appears:
Expand learning vertically, not horizontally(repeating).
Examples:
Add real-life example
Ask students to predict outcome
Let students explain concept themselves
Give quick challenge question
The concept becomes deeper.

Failure Scenario.1
The instructor is explaining a concept.
Suddenly, Zoom freezes, and the instructor disconnects.
After reconnecting, the instructor immediately continues the explanation
from where they stopped
What Failed:
Students confused because the
context was lost and they may missed
parts of explaination.

-- 22 of 37 --

When reconnecting, the instructor rebuilds the
context first:
Confirm audio/video works.
Briefly recap the last key idea.
Continue the explanation.
Example:
“It looks like the connection dropped. Let’s quickly
recap the last step before we continue.”
Disconnect During Explanation
Recovery
control

-- 23 of 37 --

Failure Scenario.2
The instructor is explaining a concept.
A parent suddenly joins the session and says:
“Excuse me, I need to ask you something about my child.”
Instructor immediately starts discussing the issue with the parent.
What Failed:
The instructor allowed the interruption
to take control of the session.
The learning flow stopped.

-- 24 of 37 --

Instructor protects the session while
acknowledging the parent.
Example response:
“Thank you for letting me know. I’ll be happy to
discuss this with you after the session so we don’t
interrupt the students' learning.”
Then the instructor returns to the session

-- 34 of 37 --

A professional instructor analyzes the session
differently:
Instead of asking:
“Why is the student weak?”
They ask:
“What could I do differently to engage the student?”
Example reflection:
“The student was hesitant to answer. I could guide
them with smaller questions or simpler steps.”
This shows ownership of the session.
