=== SLIDE 10 ===
Planning for the lesson  
Effective planning is about more than just knowing the content; it's about
anticipating student needs and building a dynamic lesson.
Define the Lesson Flow
Understand Objectives: Clearly define the lesson's goals.
Plan Pacing: Time each section and ensure smooth transitions.
Anticipate Challenges: Be ready to explain difficult topics in different
ways
The tutor should brainstorm about the content and questions the
students may ask

=== SLIDE 11 ===
Plan for Possible Scenarios
Student already knows the content
Effect: “Learn” part may feel repetitive.
Plan: Shorten but keep it engaging, keep explaining (no skipping), maintain
encouragement, 
Student takes a long time to understand
Effect: Session time might not be enough to cover all parts..
Plan: Use active engagement, connect concepts to what they already know, ask
simple questions they can answer

=== SLIDE 15 ===
Session Flow 
Welcome and Icebreaking
Warmly welcome the student and start with a brief icebreaker to create a comfortable
atmosphere. 
HW Review and Recap
Let the student explain the previous concepts.
If they struggle, give hints (e.g., remind them of an example you used before).
Ask simple, direct questions to help them recall 
Motivate them with positive feedback 
After they finish, give a quick, clear definition of each concept.

=== SLIDE 16 ===
Explain New Concepts
Brainstorming :
Before introducing a new concept, we start with a short brainstorming activity. This energizes the student,
activates relevant prior knowledge, and creates a positive, curious mindset that makes learning smoother
and more engaging.
How to do it?
Begin with a general, open-ended question about the topic to spark curiosity.
Let the student share any ideas no right or wrong at this stage.
Listen carefully to understand their thinking.
Ask follow-up questions to expand their thoughts.
Use their ideas to bridge into the topic, showing how their thoughts connect and encouraging their
participation.

=== SLIDE 19 ===
General Skills for Explaining 
Clarity and Simplicity
Break concepts into smaller, easy-to-digest parts.
Avoid jargon unless you explain it right away.
Use the Right Examples
Pick examples that match the student’s age, interests, and prior knowledge.
Make abstract concepts relatable through real-life situations.
Adapt to the Student’s Thinking
Regularly check for understanding through quick questions or feedback.
Adjust your explanation style if they look confused or disengaged.

=== SLIDE 20 ===
Support with Visuals via Annotation
Use annotation tools on the screen to draw and highlight the key ideas.
Connect All Concepts Together
Link each new concept to what the student already knows.
Show how the concept fits into the overall topic or goal.
Help the student see the purpose and flow of the lesson.
General Skills for Explaining

=== SLIDE 21 ===
As each curriculum is tailored to a specific age range. Your job is to
plan your delivery to meet their specific needs and keep them
engaged.
How to use the Right Examples
Ages 6-10: Focus on storytelling, and very short, hands-on activities to hold
their attention.
Ages 11-14: Motivate them with challenges and  Encourage them to work
independently on a task and present their solution, fostering a sense of
accomplishment.
Ages 15-18: Discuss how the principles they're learning are used in professional
careers and industry-standard projects.

=== SLIDE 22 ===
Project and Make
Start with motivation: Show the final result of the project first to spark students’
interest and encourage them.
Break it down: Divide the project into small segments, each containing 2–3 steps.
Demonstrate first: Show and explain the first 2–3 steps on your screen before
students try them.
Student implementation: Guide the students to repeat the same steps on their
own devices.
Continuous guidance: Stay involved, offering step-by-step guidance and
encouragement, don’t leave them to work without your support.
Project Personalization: Try to connect the lesson topic to the student’s personal
interests. For example, if the student likes football, design programming examples
around that theme to increase their engagement and motivation.

=== SLIDE 28 ===
Submit the feedback by selecting a suitable rating for each criterion based on the
student’s performance. Write the comment in English, even for Arabic sessions.
Criteria of feedback submission:
If the student got 5 stars in all criteria, choose a positive English comment.
If not, choose an English comment explaining why they didn’t get full stars.
If the student was absent, use the toggle to mark them as “Absent.”
The maximum time to submit session feedback is 2 hours.
Avoid repeating the same comments for all students or using the same
comment for a student across different sessions.
Personalized Feedback: Instead of relying on generic templates, mention a
specific moment where the student performed well during the session in your
feedback. This gives parents a clear impression of your genuine attention to
their child’s progress.
